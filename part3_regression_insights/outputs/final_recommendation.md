# Final Recommendation

## Overview

The objective of this analysis was to identify the factors most strongly associated with monthly sales performance across retail stores using regression analysis. Both simple regression and multiple regression models were evaluated, and the multiple regression model was selected as the final model because it provided higher explanatory power and better business insights.

## Which factors appear most strongly associated with monthly sales?

The regression analysis indicates that the following variables have the strongest association with monthly sales:

1. Marketing Spend
2. Footfall
3. Inventory Availability Percentage

These variables showed positive coefficients in the regression models, suggesting that increases in these variables are generally associated with higher monthly sales.

The multiple regression model also achieved a higher R-squared value compared to the simple regression models, indicating that these variables together explain a larger proportion of the variation in monthly sales.

## Which variables should leadership focus on?

Leadership should primarily focus on:

### Marketing Spend

Higher marketing investment is associated with higher monthly sales. Targeted marketing campaigns and optimized advertising budgets may help improve store performance.

### Footfall

Customer traffic showed a strong positive relationship with sales performance. Increasing store visits through promotions, local events, and customer engagement initiatives may generate additional revenue.

### Inventory Availability

Stores with higher inventory availability generally achieved stronger sales performance. Reducing stock-outs and improving inventory planning should remain a strategic priority.

## Which variables should not be over-interpreted?

Variables with high p-values or weak coefficients should not be interpreted as major business drivers.

Examples may include:

* Average Discount Percentage
* Customer Rating
* Certain Store Type dummy variables
* Certain Region dummy variables

Although these variables may influence sales in some situations, the regression results do not provide strong enough evidence to treat them as primary sales drivers.

## What business action would you recommend?

Based on the regression evidence, the following actions are recommended:

* Increase investment in effective marketing activities.
* Improve inventory forecasting and replenishment processes.
* Focus on initiatives that increase customer footfall.
* Monitor store-level performance regularly to identify underperforming stores.
* Use discounts selectively rather than relying heavily on discounting strategies.

These actions are expected to improve sales performance while maintaining operational efficiency.

## What risks or limitations should leadership keep in mind?

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the model results:

* The model does not include competitor activity.
* Seasonal effects may influence sales performance.
* Economic conditions and local demographics are not included.
* Promotional campaigns outside normal marketing spend may affect sales.
* Store management quality and customer preferences are not directly measured.

Therefore, the model should be used as a decision-support tool rather than as the only basis for strategic decisions.

## Why does regression show association but not automatically prove causation?

Regression analysis measures statistical relationships between variables. A strong relationship between two variables does not automatically mean that one variable causes the other.

For example, higher marketing spend may be associated with higher sales, but it is also possible that stores with stronger sales receive larger marketing budgets.

Similarly, higher footfall may contribute to higher sales, but successful stores may naturally attract more customers.

Because of this, regression results should be combined with business knowledge, operational experience, and controlled experiments before making major strategic decisions.

## Final Recommendation

The multiple regression model was selected as the final model because it provides stronger explanatory power than individual simple regression models and captures the combined effect of multiple business drivers.

The analysis suggests that leadership should prioritize marketing effectiveness, customer traffic generation, and inventory availability management, as these factors appear to have the strongest association with monthly sales performance.

The findings from this analysis can support data-driven decision-making while acknowledging that additional business factors may also influence sales outcomes.
